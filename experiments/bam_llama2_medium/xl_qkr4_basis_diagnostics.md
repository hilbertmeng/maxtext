# XL LocalQK rank-4 basis sharing diagnostic

Status: raw-basis and bias decomposition complete on all 128 sequences. Repeated raw
statistics and losses match exactly; diagnostic TPU retained per user request.

## Question and scope

Can the four Q bases and four K bases be approximately represented by four shared
bases? Analyze row and column independently, at every layer and every valid token.
This XL Head16×128 model uses full M[64,32], not Medium M[32,32]. LocalQK row keys
have dimension64 and column keys dimension32; fetch/LocalO instead use M[64,8].
Capture projection plus its learned pre-RMS bias (`Wx+b`); exclude normalization,
gates and head mixing. Head mixing combines bases and does not rotate their native
coordinate system. Subspace overlap is a geometric bound, not evidence that an
equally cheap shared projection can realize the trained function.

Metrics: signed and absolute 4×4 Q–K cosine matrices; separate Q/K singular spectra;
joint eight-basis spectra before and after unit normalization; bidirectional span
coverage; token-centered comparisons. Save per-sequence statistics before aggregation.
Raw-energy and unit-direction spectra answer different questions and are both retained.
L0 has zero bases and undefined cosine/span interpretations; exclude it from summaries.

## Results

Full-cohort [layer/side tables](xl_qkr4_basis_layer_summary.md) are available.
The full 4×4 matrices and spectra are in [layer_details.md](/data0/xd/bam_diagnostics/xl-qkr4-basis-16000/layer_details.md).
Each `batch_NNN.npz` contains one sequence's statistics;
`metadata.json` records all sequence hashes. `summary.md` is generated by the committed
analysis script. Partial summaries must not be labeled complete.

- Row: joint rank4 retains 68.31–86.56%; L1 is most redundant. From L9 onward,
  Q-in-K span coverage is mostly 6.9–13.4%, versus 34.2% at L1. Broad late-layer
  row-basis sharing is not supported as a near-lossless compression.
- Column: joint rank4 retains 79.65–87.21%; L2/L4/L5/L7 have substantially greater
  cross-span overlap (Q-in-K 42.6–45.6%) than late L12/L13/L16/L23 (12.5–14.6%).
- These side differences cannot all be called learned redundancy: row dimension64
  versus column dimension32 gives random isotropic rank4 span coverage 6.25% versus
  12.5%. Several late layers approach those geometric reference levels.
- High joint rank4 energy alone does not prove Q/K share directions. Example L23
  column retains 86.67%, yet Q-in-K coverage is only 12.48%; internal anisotropy and
  unequal magnitudes can concentrate energy without shared Q/K subspaces.
- This diagnoses key geometry, not readout error or training loss. Do not translate
  a retained-energy percentage into an asserted loss delta.

## Bias decomposition extension

Complete [per-layer/per-side bias tables](xl_qkr4_bias_layer_summary.md) and
[all four bases separately](/data0/xd/bam_diagnostics/xl-qkr4-bias-16000/layer_details.md)
retain Q/K distinctions. The 128 paired samples pass exact metadata, loss and raw-stage
validation. Main findings (layers1–23; layer0 is zero):

| Quantity, range across layers and Q/K | Row (64-dimensional keys) | Column (32-dimensional keys) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean bias norm / mean dynamic norm | 0.571–1.821% | 1.356–2.809% |
| Mean cosine of Wx and b | .115–.869 | .616–.937 |
| Increase in joint rank4 retained energy from bias | .042–.278 percentage points | .025–.248 percentage points |

Bias is not the principal source of observed Q/K low-rank structure: the structure
remains in Wx. Its small magnitude must not be confused with an absence of training
utility. Column bias is more consistently aligned with Wx and relatively larger;
row bias alignment varies much more with depth. Row L5 and column L14 show the
largest spectral changes, still below .3 percentage points. The existing evidence
does not justify near-lossless all-layer Q/K sharing or removing bias from training.
Separate key geometry from M-weighted readout error and eventual loss.
